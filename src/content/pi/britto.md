**Research Interests**

- Development of Enabling BioMedical Technologies for Accurate Design of Protein Nanomaterials
- Custom-Designed Protein Nanomaterials for Healthcare Applications including Therapeutics, Diagnostics and Vaccines

The field of biomolecular nanotechnologies has contributed significantly to the design of molecular scaffolds that are based on DNA, RNA, peptides, and carbohydrates. Among the various biomolecular nanotechnologies, protein nanotechnology is relatively new and highly challenging to practice. However, the rapid advances in synthetic gene synthesis and the highly predictive power of computational protein design have led to the design of elaborate protein structures with high accuracy. Although useful, this method often yields protein assemblies, which are static. This is in sharp contrast to natural protein assemblies, which are known to exhibit highly dynamic behavior achieved through various post-translational modifications. In addition, the computational protein design method is mostly restricted to standard 20 amino acids with very few exceptions.

On the other hand, chemical strategies provide enormous opportunities to design protein super-structures that can exhibit dynamic behavior. However, most of the chemical methods are not considered mature technologies because these methods often yield a polydisperse sample that lacks high-resolution structural information, and more importantly, these methods are restricted to a few proteins. To address these challenges, our group initiated a research program on the development of enabling biomedical technologies for accurate design of protein nanomaterials, and the use of custom-designed protein nanomaterials for healthcare applications including therapeutics, diagnostics and vaccines.
